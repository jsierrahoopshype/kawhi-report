What Steve Ballmer and the Clippers said publicly or to investigators, next to what the record showed.

They said
Report found
"They were off to the races on their own. We weren't involved." (Ballmer, September 2025)
The report finds Zucker suggested the deal, enlisted a Clippers-retained agent, conveyed the $5M + $7M terms, gave input on the term sheet and stayed in communication for months, and that Frank intervened when talks stalled. Ballmer approved the Forum Agreement knowing it was the precondition.
Aspiration asked to be introduced to Kawhi after the sponsorship was done. (Ballmer, September 2025)
The report finds Zucker raised Leonard as an example partner at the October 25, 2021 meeting she requested. Sanberg asked for help two days later.
The companies requested the June 2020 introductions. (Zucker's emails)
No documentary evidence supports it; the evidence on one company contradicts it; investigators do not credit that all three asked within six days. Zucker could not recall what prompted the emails.
The $7M-a-year Forum payment came from a consultant's $28M emissions study. (Ballmer and Zucker, to investigators)
The consultant told investigators the Clippers gave him a $28M budget, not the other way around. The $7M appeared in the first draft as "$7M back in business" before any analysis.
Zucker told Sanberg the NBA rules prevented her from helping. (Zucker, to investigators)
The report finds that in the same conversation she offered to enlist a business agent to help structure the deal, and did so the next day.
Introductions are "ordinary practice." (Clippers statement, August 2026)
The NBA's guidance explicitly lists a team recommending a player to a sponsor as prohibited. The only exception is supplying contact information when a partner asks. Ballmer, Zucker and Frank each described the rules correctly in interviews.
The Clippers' position, as HoopsMatic summarizes it (not a quoted claim): the endorsements were ordinary marketing deals that companies chose on their own.
The report finds the deals were never announced, barely activated, signed mid-pandemic by companies that had never done anything of the kind, with minimal obligations, and that each was initiated, facilitated and induced by the Clippers. Aspiration's executives called Kawhi "not a big name" and expected Clippers business to offset the cost.
