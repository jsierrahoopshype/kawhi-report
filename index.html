Kawhi Leonard / Clippers report

Download the report (PDF)

On September 2, 2026, the NBA released Wachtell Lipton's summary findings on the Clippers, Steve Ballmer and Kawhi Leonard. This section breaks the 35-page report into its findings, the timeline, the people and companies, the money, every quoted email and text, and the rules. Published Sept. 3, 2026

The five violations

  1. i
    Initiated off-court income

    The report finds the Clippers affirmatively initiated endorsement opportunities between Leonard and four companies doing business with the team: Aspiration, Boingo, Daktronics and Lockton.

  2. ii
    Facilitated the deals

    The report finds the team facilitated the agreements, in some cases by participating in the development and communication of deal terms.

  3. iii
    Induced with team business

    The report finds the Clippers induced the companies to sign by offering and providing them with Clippers business: consulting agreements, arena contracts, the Forum sustainability deal.

  4. iv
    Paid personal expenses

    The report finds hundreds of instances of team-paid air and ground travel, accommodations, gifts and tickets for Leonard, his family and Dennis Robertson, without the deductions from Leonard's pay that the CBA requires.

  5. v
    Failed to report solicitations

    The report finds no evidence the Clippers ever reported Robertson's improper solicitations, as required by a 2019 rule that was itself created because of Robertson's conduct during Leonard's free agency.

Who is held responsible

Gillian ZuckerPresident of Business Operations

Primarily and directly culpable

The report finds she was the point person on all four deals and directly initiated, facilitated and induced each endorsement agreement, and that she made "misleading and false statements to investigators" about her and the Clippers' role.

Lawrence FrankPresident of Basketball Operations

Primarily and directly culpable

The report finds he was Robertson's primary team contact, conveyed Robertson's improper demands to Ballmer and Zucker, and was ultimately responsible for approving the impermissible expense payments. Investigators describe him as open, consistent and willing to take responsibility.

Steve BallmerOwner

Knowingly sought to help

The report finds he knowingly sought to help Leonard obtain off-court income and engaged in "a significant act of team facilitation" by personally approving the Forum Agreement in April 2022 while aware that Sanberg had made it a precondition of the Kawhi deal. It also finds he failed to supervise Zucker and permitted a culture that allowed the deals to happen.

Kawhi LeonardPlayer

Violated through Robertson

The report finds that Leonard, through Robertson's conduct on his behalf, violated the circumvention rules by pressuring the Clippers over many years to assist him in obtaining off-court income, successfully obtaining it, and failing to reimburse the team for personal expenses not authorized by the CBA.

Dennis RobertsonLeonard's uncle and business manager

Made the improper demands

The report finds he communicated a target of approximately $10 million per year in outside income and pressured Frank, Ballmer and Zucker to deliver it, and that many of the personal expenses the team paid were requested by him.

The four deals$66M to Kawhi Leonard

Boingo WirelessWireless and communications networks

Signed: July-August 2020. The report does not specify which of Boingo or Lockton was the second early-July agreement.
To Kawhi: Part of the $18M paid under the three 2020 deals
Clippers business and related agreements: A multi-million dollar consulting agreement with the Clippers

The report says Boingo purported initially to cooperate, then supplied information investigators found inconsistent or not credible, and ultimately refused to cooperate further.

DaktronicsScoreboards and video displays

Signed: July 6, 2020 (amended May 20, 2021)
To Kawhi: $3M per year for two years, plus $2M added to the second year
Clippers business and related agreements: Preferred-provider status (May 2020) for the Intuit Dome scoreboard and signage contract

The report states its conclusion explicitly here: the Daktronics deal was procured by the Clippers in exchange for team business. A senior Clippers executive specified the terms as a "spend back," and in February 2021 the same executive told Daktronics to raise Leonard's pay because the team was spending more on the scoreboard. Daktronics cooperated substantially.

Lockton InsuranceInsurance brokerage

Signed: July-August 2020. The report does not specify which of Boingo or Lockton was the second early-July agreement.
To Kawhi: Part of the $18M paid under the three 2020 deals
Clippers business and related agreements: A multi-million dollar consulting agreement with the Clippers

Lockton refused to cooperate with investigators. Separately, the report says Zucker had personal ties at two of the three 2020 companies (her husband chaired one board and she had a 30-year relationship with that CEO; at another she had a longstanding relationship with the president who signed the Kawhi deal), without naming which companies.

Aspiration PartnersSustainability services, now bankrupt; co-founder Joe Sanberg is a convicted felon

Signed: Negotiated from October 2021. The report does not give a signing date.
To Kawhi: $48M: $7M cash + $5M equity per year for four years
Clippers business and related agreements: The $7M-a-year Forum Agreement (April 2022), which Sanberg had made a precondition of the Kawhi deal. Separately, in September 2021: a 23-year, $382.5M jersey patch and founding arena partner agreement; a 23-year, $72M agreement for Aspiration to provide sustainability services to the Intuit Dome; and a $50M personal investment by Ballmer in Aspiration.

Aspiration's own executives called Kawhi "not a big name" and questioned the deal. Contemporaneous emails show they understood that additional Clippers business would offset the Kawhi payments ("We should be fine if it's cashflow neutral"). Aspiration cooperated through its bankruptcy trustee.

What the report is not

Scope

A summary, not the full evidentiary record. It does not set penalties; the NBA does that separately. It leaves out public theories the evidence did not support. Investigators say information is still arriving, including "as recently as this week," so supplements are possible.

Method

73 interviews of 60 people and more than 200,000 pages of documents. The Clippers were required to cooperate; outside companies were not. Lockton refused, Boingo stopped, Aspiration (through its bankruptcy trustee) and Daktronics cooperated substantially.
