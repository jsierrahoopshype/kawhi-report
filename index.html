HOOPSHYPE

On September 2, 2026, the NBA released Wachtell Lipton's summary findings on the Clippers, Steve Ballmer and Kawhi Leonard. This section breaks the 35-page report into its findings, the timeline, the people and companies, the money, every quoted email and text, and the rules. Published Sept. 3, 2026

The five violations

  1. i
    Initiated off-court income

    The Clippers affirmatively initiated endorsement opportunities between Leonard and four companies doing business with the team: Aspiration, Boingo, Daktronics and Lockton.

  2. ii
    Facilitated the deals

    The team helped the agreements get done, in some cases by developing and communicating the deal terms themselves.

  3. iii
    Induced with team business

    The companies signed because the Clippers offered and delivered business back: consulting fees, arena contracts, the Forum sustainability deal.

  4. iv
    Paid personal expenses

    Hundreds of instances of flights, ground travel, hotels, gifts and tickets for Leonard, his family and Dennis Robertson, never deducted from Leonard's pay as the CBA requires.

  5. v
    Failed to report solicitations

    Robertson's repeated demands for benefits outside the CBA were never reported to the league, as required by a 2019 rule that was itself created because of Robertson's conduct during Leonard's free agency.

Who is held responsible

Gillian ZuckerPresident of Business Operations

Primarily and directly culpable

Point person on all four deals. Directly initiated, facilitated and induced each endorsement agreement. Made "misleading and false statements to investigators" about her role.

Lawrence FrankPresident of Basketball Operations

Primarily and directly culpable

Robertson's primary contact. Conveyed his improper demands to Ballmer and Zucker. Approved the impermissible expense payments. Cooperative and consistent with investigators.

Steve BallmerOwner

Knowingly sought to help

Sought to help Leonard obtain off-court income. Personally approved the Forum Agreement knowing Sanberg had made it a precondition of the Kawhi deal, which the report calls "a significant act of team facilitation." Failed to supervise Zucker.

Kawhi LeonardPlayer

Violated through Robertson

Through Robertson's conduct, pressured the Clippers for years to assist with off-court income, obtained it, and failed to reimburse personal expenses the team paid.

Dennis RobertsonLeonard's uncle and business manager

Made the improper demands

Set a target of roughly $10 million per year in outside income and pressured Frank, Ballmer and Zucker. Requested many of the personal expenses the team covered.

The four deals$66M to Kawhi Leonard

Boingo WirelessWireless and communications networks

Signed: July 2020
To Kawhi: Part of $18M
Business from the Clippers: Multi-million consulting agreement with the Clippers

Signed the same day as one other deal, within a month of Zucker's June 2020 "introduction." Purported to cooperate, then supplied information investigators found not credible and stopped cooperating.

DaktronicsScoreboards and video displays

Signed: July 6, 2020 (amended May 20, 2021)
To Kawhi: $3M/yr x 2, plus $2M added in year two
Business from the Clippers: Intuit Dome scoreboard and signage contract (preferred provider status, May 2020)

The clearest case. A senior Clippers executive dictated the terms as a "spend back." In Feb. 2021 the same executive came back for $2M more because the team was spending more on the scoreboard. Cooperated substantially.

Lockton InsuranceInsurance brokerage

Signed: By end of August 2020
To Kawhi: Part of $18M
Business from the Clippers: Multi-million consulting agreement with the Clippers

Refused to cooperate with investigators. Separately, the report says Zucker had personal ties at two of the three 2020 companies (her husband chaired one board and she had a 30-year relationship with that CEO; at another she was close to the president who signed the Kawhi deal), without naming which.

Aspiration PartnersSustainability services, now bankrupt; co-founder Joe Sanberg is a convicted felon

Signed: Negotiated Oct. 2021 to spring 2022
To Kawhi: $48M: $7M cash + $5M equity per year for four years
Business from the Clippers: $382.5M jersey patch and founding arena partner deal (23 years), $72M sustainability services deal (23 years), $50M personal Ballmer investment, then the $7M/yr Forum Agreement

Aspiration's own C-suite called Kawhi "not a big name" and only agreed because Sanberg said the Clippers would send business back to make it "cashflow neutral." Cooperated via its bankruptcy trustee.

What the report is not

Scope

A summary, not the full evidentiary record. It does not set penalties; the NBA does that separately. It leaves out public theories the evidence did not support. Investigators say information is still arriving, including "as recently as this week," so supplements are possible.

Method

73 interviews of 60 people and more than 200,000+ pages of documents. The Clippers were required to cooperate; outside companies were not. Lockton refused, Boingo stopped, Aspiration (through its bankruptcy trustee) and Daktronics cooperated substantially.
