What the report says about each

Kawhi LeonardClippers player, signed July 2019, re-signed August 2021
137 mentions

Received $18M under the three 2020 deals by August 2021; the Aspiration agreement required $48M over four years. The report says his only confirmed activity under the three 2020 agreements was a single military base visit under one and signing some memorabilia under another. He was out for 2021-22 with a torn right ACL when the Aspiration deal was drafted. Represented by NBPA counsel. The report finds he violated the rules through Robertson's conduct and by failing to reimburse team-paid personal expenses.

Gillian ZuckerClippers President of Business Operations
56 mentions

Credibility: Low

Sent the June 2020 and November 2021 "introduction" emails, which the report finds were written to appear responsive to company requests that investigators did not credit. The report concludes she conveyed the Aspiration deal terms to a business agent under Clippers retention, and that she suggested the Daktronics spend-back take the form of a Kawhi endorsement. Investigators say she made statements inconsistent with the documents, professed lack of recollection, placed responsibility on subordinates, and made "misleading and false statements."

Dennis RobertsonLeonard's uncle and business manager; a member of KL2 LBS LLC with Leonard
37 mentions

Requested equity, housing, private transportation and guaranteed endorsement income during 2019 free agency. Communicated a target of approximately $10M per year in Clippers-assisted outside income. Per Frank's notes, complained in April 2020 that Zucker's intros were for "bull**** deals" and that "I have to get paid." KL2 LBS LLC, whose members were Leonard and Robertson, was filed June 9, 2020, the day of Zucker's second intro email.

Joe SanbergAspiration co-founder and board member, later convicted of fraud
36 mentions

Credibility: Used with caution, relied on when corroborated

Asked Zucker for help exploring a Kawhi deal two days after she raised the idea. Told his own executives "the Clippers are asking us to do this." In March 2022 threatened to "tear up the Clippers contract" and tell Kawhi the deal was dead unless the Forum Agreement was signed. Told investigators the $7M Forum figure was tied to Aspiration's contemplated Kawhi payments, which the report says is corroborated by his own February 2022 email.

Steve BallmerClippers owner
32 mentions

Credibility: Public statements found "inaccurate (at best)"

Per Frank's notes, told Robertson in April 2020 the organization were "collective workers to try to help [Leonard] achieve his financial goals." Made a $50M personal investment in Aspiration. Told investigators he was aware of Sanberg's threat, and the record reflects he personally approved the Forum Agreement in April 2022. The report finds his September 2025 statement that the Clippers "weren't involved" beyond an intro email is inaccurate at best.

Lawrence FrankClippers President of Basketball Operations
18 mentions

Credibility: High: open, consistent, took responsibility

Robertson's primary team contact. Kept the contemporaneous notes of the April 2020 calls that the report relies on. Responsible for authorizing the personal expense payments. Asked Zucker to get involved when Frankel complained in February 2022 that Aspiration had gone quiet.

Eric ChanClippers Chief Financial Officer at the time
6 mentions

Named in the first Forum term sheet as the person to fill in "$7M back in business." Wrote to Ballmer's personal business office on March 30-31, 2022 that Sanberg would "burn everything down" and that "Steve knows" Sanberg would call Kawhi if the Forum deal collapsed.

Scott SonnenbergClippers Chief Commercial Officer
4 mentions

Texted Chan in March 2022: "I never wanted this Forum deal. Always knew it was super shady," and in another exchange, "I cannot begin to tell you all the red flags with these deals. Just doing what I'm told at this point." Sanberg's texts say Sonnenberg asked him not to break the Kawhi deal.

Mitch FrankelLeonard's certified player agent
4 mentions

Included in Zucker's November 4, 2021 pre-introduction call. Texted Frank in February 2022 asking for help with an unresponsive Aspiration, then thanked him: "we are engaged again."

Alex WinsbergClippers General Counsel at the time
2 mentions

Named in Sanberg's March 30, 2022 texts as having "no solution" to the Forum Agreement standoff.

Brandt VaughanChief Investment Officer of Ballmer's personal business office
1 mentions

Recipient of Chan's March 30 and 31, 2022 messages about Sanberg's threats.

DeAndre JordanFormer Clippers center
1 mentions

The 2015 precedent. The Clippers tried to facilitate an endorsement between Jordan and an incoming sponsor during his free agency and were fined $250,000.

Pablo TorreHost of Pablo Torre Finds Out
1 mentions

His September 3, 2025 episode revealed the Aspiration deal and led the league to hire Wachtell Lipton.

The business agentUnnamed; under a retention agreement with the Clippers
not named in the report

Enlisted by Zucker on October 28, 2021 to help Sanberg structure the Kawhi offer. His internal emails ("Aspiration and Kawhi," "I reviewed with club to get input") are the core documentary evidence on the Aspiration deal. Told investigators neither he nor his team came up with the $5M + $7M terms.
