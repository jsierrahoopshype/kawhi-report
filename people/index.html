What the report says about each

Kawhi LeonardClippers player; signed July 2019, re-signed August 2021
142 mentions

Received $18M under the three 2020 agreements by August 2021; the Aspiration agreement required $48M over four years. The report says his only confirmed activity under the three 2020 agreements was a visit to a military base on a single occasion under one and signing some memorabilia under another. He was out for the 2021-22 season with a torn right ACL. Represented in the investigation by NBPA counsel. The report finds that, through Robertson's conduct on his behalf, he violated the circumvention rules by pressuring the Clippers to assist him in obtaining off-court income, obtaining it, and failing to reimburse team-paid personal expenses not authorized by the CBA.

Gillian ZuckerClippers President of Business Operations
57 mentions

Investigators' assessment: Significant credibility concerns

Sent the June 2020 introduction emails to Boingo, Daktronics and Lockton, whose claimed company-initiated requests investigators did not credit. In November 2021, after Sanberg asked her for help exploring a Kawhi endorsement, she sent the formal Aspiration introduction email that investigators concluded was drafted solely for record-making purposes, after the dealmaking process was already underway. The report concludes she conveyed the proposed Aspiration deal terms to a business agent under Clippers retention, and says she suggested to a Daktronics executive that the spend-back could take the form of a Kawhi endorsement. Investigators found several of her statements inconsistent with contemporaneous documents, other witnesses and the broader chronology; she professed lack of recollection on important issues, placed responsibility on subordinates, and gave inconsistent accounts across interviews. The report finds she was the point person on all four deals and made misleading and false statements to investigators about her and the Clippers' role.

Dennis RobertsonLeonard's uncle and business manager; a member of KL2 LBS LLC with Leonard
40 mentions

During 2019 free agency, requested benefits prohibited under the CBA, including equity, housing, private transportation and off-court income such as endorsement deals, from the Clippers and other teams; the report says this is not disputed. Communicated to the Clippers a target of approximately $10 million per year in outside income. Per Frank's contemporaneous notes, complained to Ballmer in April 2020 that Zucker's intros were for "bull**** deals" and that "I have to get paid." KL2 LBS LLC, whose members were Leonard and Robertson, was filed June 9, 2020, the day of the second intro email. The report finds the Clippers never reported his solicitations to the NBA as required, and that many of the personal expenses the team paid were requested by him.

Joe SanbergAspiration co-founder and board member; the report notes he is now a convicted felon for fraud while leading that company
36 mentions

Investigators' assessment: Used with caution; relied upon where corroborated

Two days after Zucker raised sponsor-player endorsements with him, told her he wanted to explore an endorsement agreement with Kawhi and asked for her help. Did not have authority to sign such an agreement for Aspiration. Told Aspiration's executives that "the Clippers are asking us to do this with Kawhi Leonard" and that the team would provide additional business back. In March 2022 texted that he would "tear up the Clippers contract" and tell Kawhi and Robertson the deal was off unless the Forum Agreement was signed. Told investigators the $7 million Forum figure was tied to Aspiration's contemplated Kawhi payments; the report says his February 2022 email corroborates this.

Steve BallmerClippers owner
32 mentions

Aspiration account: Found "inaccurate (at best)"

Per Frank's contemporaneous notes, told Robertson in April 2020 that he and Clippers personnel were "collective workers to try to help [Leonard] achieve his financial goals." Made a $50 million personal investment in Aspiration in September 2021. Told investigators he was aware of Sanberg's threat not to proceed with the Kawhi deal unless the Forum Agreement was executed; the record reflects he personally approved that agreement in April 2022, which the report finds was an act of facilitation that violates the rules. The report finds his September 2025 televised statements that the Clippers' only involvement with Aspiration was an introductory email were inaccurate at best, and that he failed to supervise Zucker and permitted a culture in which the deals could happen.

Lawrence FrankClippers President of Basketball Operations
18 mentions

Investigators' assessment: Generally consistent and cooperative

Robertson's primary contact at the team. Kept the contemporaneous notes of the April 2020 conversations that the report relies on. The report finds he conveyed Robertson's improper demands to Ballmer and Zucker, was ultimately responsible for approving the impermissible expense payments, and failed to report Robertson's solicitations. Told investigators he asked Zucker to get involved after Frankel complained in February 2022 that Aspiration was unresponsive. Investigators say he openly discussed his conduct, recalled key details, took responsibility for the actions of subordinates and was generally consistent across interviews.

Eric ChanClippers Chief Financial Officer at the time
6 mentions

Named in the initial Forum term sheet as the person who would fill in the "Business Back" heading ("$7M back in business"). Wrote to Brandt Vaughan on March 30-31, 2022 that Sanberg would "sue Steve, the Clippers, and burn everything down" and that "Steve knows" Sanberg would call Kawhi if the Forum deal collapsed. The report cites these messages as confirming Clippers executives were aware of the linkage between the Forum Agreement and the Kawhi deal.

Scott SonnenbergClippers Chief Commercial Officer
4 mentions

Texted Chan in March 2022: "I never wanted this Forum deal. Always knew it was super shady," and in another exchange, "I cannot begin to tell you all the red flags with these deals. Just doing what I'm told at this point." In a March 30, 2022 text, Sanberg wrote that Sonnenberg had asked him not to break the Kawhi deal.

Mitch FrankelLeonard's certified player agent
4 mentions

Zucker requested a November 4, 2021 pre-introduction call with Frankel and Robertson to discuss Aspiration. Texted Frank in February 2022 complaining that Aspiration was unresponsive and asking for help, then thanked him: "we are engaged again."

Alex WinsbergClippers General Counsel at the time
2 mentions

Named in Sanberg's March 30, 2022 texts as having, with Chan, "no solution" to the Forum Agreement standoff.

Brandt VaughanChief Investment Officer of Ballmer's personal business office at the time

Recipient of Chan's March 30 and 31, 2022 messages about Sanberg's threats.

DeAndre JordanFormer Clippers center
1 mentions

The 2015 precedent: the NBA found the Clippers engaged in efforts to facilitate an endorsement agreement between Jordan, then a free-agent target, and an incoming team sponsor, and fined the team $250,000.

Pablo TorreHost of the podcast Pablo Torre Finds Out
1 mentions

His September 3, 2025 episode revealed the Aspiration agreement and aired an anonymous person's allegation about its purpose; the league hired Wachtell Lipton shortly after.

The business agentNot named in the summary report; described as under a retention agreement with the Clippers
not named in the report

Enlisted by Zucker on October 28, 2021 to help Sanberg structure the Kawhi offer. His internal emails ("Aspiration and Kawhi," "I reviewed with club to get input") are the documentary evidence the report relies on for the Aspiration deal terms. Told investigators that neither he nor his internal team came up with those terms.
