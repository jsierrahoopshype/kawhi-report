The NBA cap circumvention rules behind the Kawhi Leonard / Clippers case

Download the report (PDF)
HOOPSHYPE

What cap circumvention means, why a team "introduction" can be a violation, and the rule that exists because of Kawhi Leonard's own 2019 free agency.

What circumvention means

The circumvention rules broadly prohibit teams and players (including their representatives, relatives and affiliates) from arranging, promising, soliciting or attempting to provide compensation, business opportunities or other things of value outside the player's authorized Uniform Player Contract. The ask itself can be a violation: the CBA bars "attempt[ing]" to enter into or "solicit[ing]" such arrangements.

The exact language

Prohibited are agreements or transactions involving "compensation or consideration of any kind or anything else of value, to be paid, furnished, or made available by, to, or for the benefit of the player, or any person or entity controlled by, related to, or acting with authority on behalf of the player" and "an investment or business opportunity to be furnished or made available by, to, or for the benefit of the player."

Why introductions count

The NBA's Operations Manual and annual reminder memos give two examples of prohibited conduct: "A team representative recommends one of the team's players to a team sponsor as a candidate for an endorsement arrangement" and "A team representative initiates or facilitates an endorsement relationship between a team sponsor and one of the team's players." If a partner independently asks about a player, the team may respond solely by providing the player's or agent's contact information. It may not itself recommend, initiate or facilitate the relationship.

The Kawhi rule

After Robertson's 2019 free agency requests, the NBA adopted a requirement that teams report any solicitation of unauthorized benefits by a player, agent or anyone acting for the player, even if the team says no. The report finds no evidence the Clippers ever reported Robertson's demands. This is violation (v).

Personal expenses

Subject to specific CBA exceptions, teams generally cannot pay personal expenses for players, their representatives or family. Where they do, the amount must be deducted from the player's pay. The report finds the Clippers paid hundreds of such expenses without deducting them.

What the report does not decide

Penalties. Wachtell Lipton makes findings; the NBA determines consequences. Investigators recommend the league weigh cooperation and credibility. The report also excludes public theories the evidence did not substantiate, and says more information is still coming in.

Three levels of certainty in the report

Firm findings: the Clippers initiated, facilitated and induced the four deals, paid unauthorized expenses, and failed to report Robertson's solicitations. Evidence short of a conclusion: that Clippers payments may have funded the endorsements, based on the timing of the consulting fees and a witness account. Provisional: one credible witness with direct knowledge said a consulting agreement was a ruse to route Clippers money to Leonard; that came late and was still being corroborated. Sanberg is a convicted fraudster and investigators relied on him only where corroborated. Lockton refused to cooperate and Boingo stopped, so the record on those two deals is thinner than on Daktronics.
